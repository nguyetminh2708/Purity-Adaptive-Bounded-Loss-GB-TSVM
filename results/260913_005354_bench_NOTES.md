# Speed benchmark (linear kernel, noise 0.3, 10 datasets x 3 seeds x 5 folds)

Untuned params (svm C=10, GB d=0.1, wave lam0=1 kappa=2, 300 Adam steps).
Time = ball generation + solver. Averaged over all folds/datasets.
File: 260913_005354_bench_linear.csv

## Mean over 10 datasets (ms/fit)

| model | gen | solve | train total | predict | balls |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| svm | 0 | 210 | 210 | 0.23 | - |
| hard | 336 | 18.9 | 355 | 0.04 | 325 |
| binom | 13 | 1.7 | 14 | 0.03 | 10 |
| wave | 336 | 20.7 | 357 | 0.03 | 325 |
| wave2 | 13 | 18.1 | 31 | 0.02 | 10 |

## What the numbers say

- **The dominant cost is ball GENERATION, not the QP.** Splitting to purity=1
  makes hundreds of balls (122-752), and generation alone is 160-720 ms. The
  hard/wave variants are gen-bound; on small sets they are even slower than SVM.
- **Binomial stopping is the decisive speed lever.** Stopping early leaves only
  3-26 balls, cutting generation 10-100x AND shrinking the QP. binom trains in
  5-41 ms and wave2 in 21-58 ms *regardless of n* (flat), while SVM blows up to
  ~1150 ms at n=1097 and hard/wave sit in the hundreds.
- **Pure wave (Adam on purity=1 balls) is not a speed win** -- its solver is a
  flat ~20 ms (300 steps, independent of ball count, so it beats the QP once
  balls are many), but the purity=1 generation cost swamps that. Wave loss is a
  robustness argument, not a speed one.
- **wave2 = binomial balls + wave loss** keeps the speed (flat 21-58 ms) and
  adds only ~15 ms of Adam on top of binom. If bounded loss is used, this is
  where it is nearly free.
- Prediction is ~0 ms for all ball models (decide on a handful of planes).

Caveat for the paper: absolute times depend on the (Python) gen_balls
implementation; the *relative scaling* (binom flat vs svm/hard growing) and the
order-of-magnitude ball-count reduction are algorithmic, not implementation
artifacts.
